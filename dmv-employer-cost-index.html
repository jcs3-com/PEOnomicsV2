DMV Employer Cost Index · 2026 edition · rates reviewed Oct 27, 2026

A minimum-wage hire costs $41,790 a year in DC and $28,854 in Virginia.

That is a $12,936 gap per full-time employee, before benefits or workers’ comp. This index tracks every statutory cost an employer pays to keep one person on payroll in the District, Maryland and Virginia: federal payroll taxes, state unemployment insurance, mandated paid leave, and the wage floor itself.

Tipped staff: the restaurant gap

For tipped workers the employer pays a lower cash wage and tips make up the rest of the minimum. The cash wage is the employer’s own obligation, and it varies more across the DMV than any other line in this index.

Already enacted for 2027 and 2028

  • Jan 1, 2027 · MD

    Maryland FAMLI contributions begin at 0.90% of wages up to the Social Security wage base. Employers with 15 or more employees pay at least half (0.45%) and may withhold up to the other half from pay. Employers with fewer than 15 owe no employer share. Benefits begin by January 3, 2028.

  • Jan 1, 2027 · Federal

    The Social Security wage base rises from $184,500 to $192,300. Employer cost rises only for employees paid above $184,500.

  • Jan 1, 2027 · VA

    Virginia’s minimum wage rises from $12.77 to $13.75, then to $15.00 on January 1, 2028, under HB1 and SB1, signed April 9, 2026.

  • Oct 1, 2026 · DC

    DC paid-leave benefits were cut: family leave from 12 weeks to 6, medical leave from 12 to 10, and the maximum weekly benefit from $1,190 to $1,100. The employer contribution stayed at 0.75%, so DC employers pay the same for less coverage.

  • Jul 1, 2027 · DC

    DC’s minimum wage adjusts again for inflation. The new rate is not yet published.

Methodology

Every number on this page is a published rate multiplied by its capped wage base. There are no estimates or survey figures. Federal and unemployment-insurance rates come from the PEOnomics 2026 dataset; paid-leave and wage-floor figures from dmv-index-2026.json.

  • Unemployment insurance uses each jurisdiction’s new-employer rate so the comparison is like for like. Experienced employers get their own rate: DC 1.9% to 7.4%, Maryland 0.3% to 7.5%, Virginia base rates 0.1% to 6.2%.
  • Maryland’s 2.6% new-employer rate does not apply to new construction employers headquartered outside Maryland. Those firms are assigned the average Maryland construction-industry rate, which is never lower than the new-employer rate.
  • FUTA assumes the full 5.4% state credit. No DMV jurisdiction was a credit-reduction state for 2025.
  • The 2027 preview applies rules already enacted and holds 2026 unemployment rates, which each state sets for the coming year.
  • Not included: workers’ compensation premiums (priced by job class and carrier), health and retirement benefits, employee-paid withholding and local business taxes. These sit on top of the statutory floor measured here. The Cost Lab models them.
  • Full-time means 2,080 paid hours a year.
