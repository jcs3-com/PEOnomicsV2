    The math

    Every line, as a formula.

    N = employees, W = average annual wage. One state per model. Defaults come from the PEOnomics 2026 reference data (rates-2026.json, every figure with its source) unless you overwrite them.

    Employer FICA
    N × [min(W, SS wage base) × 6.2% + W × 1.45%]
    FUTA
    N × min(W, $7,000) × (0.6% + state credit reduction)
    SUTA
    N × min(W, state wage base) × SUTA rate
    Workers' comp
    N × W ÷ 100 × rate per $100 (state index × industry multiplier)
    Employer health
    N × enrollment % × employer cost per enrollee
    Internal admin
    Current: your figure. With PEO: your figure − what a PEO would replace (never below $0)
    PEO fee
    N × PEPM × 12 (PEO scenario only)

    No line is discounted in the PEO scenario unless you enter a PEO-quoted rate. Simplifications: one average wage for everyone (understates FICA/SUTA differences when pay is spread out), new-employer SUTA instead of your experience rate, a state-industry proxy instead of class-code workers' comp, single-coverage employer cost applied to every enrollee (family coverage costs more), health and wage benchmarks from 2025 not trended to 2026.

    How to read the result

    Three kinds of number. Labeled every time.

    A calculator is only as honest as its inputs. Each line in the lab says where its number comes from.

    Statutory

    Set by law

    Employer FICA and FUTA, with 2026 parameters. The same with or without a PEO. California's 2026 FUTA credit reduction is an estimate until DOL finalizes it in November.

    Benchmark

    Published reference

    2026 state new-employer SUTA rates, BLS state × industry wages, AHRQ state health costs and enrollment. Starting points — your actual rates will differ.

    Your input

    Your call

    Headcount, wages, the PEO fee, your internal admin cost and what a PEO would genuinely replace. Nothing here is pre-filled in a PEO's favor.

    Estimate

    Partly modeled

    Workers' comp (a sourced state index × our industry multiplier) and the few states where new employers pay an industry average. Replace with your own figure when you have it.

    Not modeled

    What this can't see

    Experience-rated SUTA, class-code workers' comp, plan design, wage distribution above the Social Security base, multi-state payroll.

    Reference desk

    Go deeper

    State rate pages, how PEO pricing works, and costs that hide in a quote.

    How information is used

    Comparison is the product.

    The calculator runs in your browser; your inputs aren't sent anywhere. If you choose the business-email prefill, we record the email, its domain, the company details we found, and the time, in a private log so we can understand how the tool is used. Only the domain — never the full address — is used for research.

    We don't sell or forward that information to PEOs, and using the lab doesn't trigger a sales call. Service providers that host the site and run the research necessarily process the request. A draft privacy notice describes this in more detail; a final policy and retention schedule will be confirmed before emailed reports launch.
